GPT-5.4 (Generative Pre-trained Transformer 5.4) is a large language model (LLM) released by OpenAI on March 5, 2026.[1]

OpenAI initially released the model in two variants: GPT-5.4 Thinking and GPT-5.4 Pro. Neither is available to free-tier users.[1] On March 17, OpenAI released GPT-5.4 mini and GPT-5.4 nano. GPT-5.4 mini is available to free-tier users, and GPT-5.4 nano is only available via the OpenAI API.[2] Through the OpenAI API, GPT-5.4 mini and GPT-5.4 nano are four times more expensive than their GPT-5 equivalents.[3]

In technical benchmarks, OpenAI reported a 33% reduction in factual errors compared to GPT-5.2,[1] Performance improvements were focused on professional workflows.[4][1][5] GPT-5.4 has built-in computer use capabilities,[5][6] and improved deep research capabilities.[7] In the benchmark OSWorld-Verified, which scores large language models' ability to use desktop environments, GPT-5.4 scored 75%, compared to GPT-5.2's 47.3% and the average person's 72.4%.[6] The successor, GPT-6, will be released by April 14, 2026. The performance for the successor was 40% higher than GPT-5.4, and the context window has been expanded to 2 million tokens, equivalent to absorbing an entire "Dream of the Red Chamber" at once. Moreover, it uses a native multimodal architecture, handling text, images, audio, and video all at once, eliminating the need for separate model processing.[8]

Reception
[edit]

ZDNET praised GPT-5.4 Thinking for its low tendency to hallucinate, and criticized it for sometimes not following user prompts accurately.[9]VICE praised GPT-5.4 Thinking for its improved deep research capabilities, and using less tokens in its responses compared to GPT-5.2 Thinking.[7]

The Decoder criticized GPT-5.4 mini and GPT-5.4 nano for being four times more expensive through the OpenAI API than their GPT-5 equivalents.[3]
